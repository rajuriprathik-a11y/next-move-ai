Interaction-Level vs. Student-Level Risk

Unlike static student proficiency ratings or cumulative GPA, student failure risk varies dynamically from question to question. Even high-performing students experience elevated failure risk when transitioning to unfamiliar, difficult topics.

Low Predicted Risk (High Probability of Success): Familiar, mastered skill on well-scaffolded exercises.
High Predicted Risk (Failure Trigger): Difficult or unfamiliar question where failure risk exceeds operating threshold (\(r_{s,t} \ge 0.2880\)).

The Educational Objective

Intelligent tutoring systems need proactive alerts. Identifying student struggle *before* incorrect submission allows platforms to offer targeted hints, recommend prerequisite reviews, or prevent frustration and churn.

Academic Scope Notice: Next Move AI models observational predictive relationships on the ASSISTments tutoring platform. It does not establish causal pedagogical mechanisms or measure overall student intelligence.
Mathematical Sequence Formulation & Formal Definitions (Click to expand)

Let \(s \in \mathcal{S}\) index a student and let \(t\) index their chronological interaction sequence, ordered globally by order_id.

  • Input Feature Vector \(\mathbf{x}_{s,t} \in \mathbb{R}^7\): Constructed strictly from student interactions occurring prior to \(t\) (\(\text{order\_id} < \text{order\_id}_{s,t}\)).
  • Binary Target \(y_{s,t} \in \{0, 1\}\): Recorded outcome on the question (\(1 =\) correct, \(0 =\) non-correct).
  • Predicted Probability of Correctness: \[\hat{p}_{s,t} = P(y_{s,t} = 1 \mid \mathbf{x}_{s,t})\]
  • Complementary Failure Risk: \[r_{s,t} = 1 - \hat{p}_{s,t} = P(y_{s,t} = 0 \mid \mathbf{x}_{s,t})\]
  • Decision Rule: The intervention alert triggers whenever \(r_{s,t} \ge \tau\), with selected operational threshold \(\tau = 0.2880\).
Information Architecture

Feature Engineering & Bayesian Smoothing

Four distinct information groups engineered with empirical Bayesian m-estimate shrinkage.

1. What It Measures

The student's global cumulative historical success rate across all previous eligible main questions, regardless of topic or skill tag.

2. Why It Matters

Captures general academic proficiency, diligence, and platform competence. Dropping G1 in ablation causes a large 0.021 ROC-AUC drop.

3. Mathematical Formula

\[\tilde{A}_{\text{overall}} = \frac{C_{\text{total}} + \alpha \cdot P_{\text{global}}}{N_{\text{total}} + \alpha}\]

Where \(\alpha = 10\) and \(P_{\text{global}} = 0.658624\) (the training-pool empirical base rate).

4. Concrete Numerical Example

Student with \(C=21\) correct out of \(N=30\) attempts: \[\tilde{A}_{\text{overall}} = \frac{21 + 10 \times 0.658624}{30 + 10} = \frac{27.5862}{40} = 0.6897 \quad (68.97\%)\]

5. Dynamic Sensitivity ("What happens when...")

• Cold-Start (\(N=0\)): Shrinks exactly to \(P_{\text{global}} = 0.658624\).
• Early Practice (\(N=3\)): Prior holds 77% of the weight, preventing premature overconfidence or panic.
• Substantial Practice (\(N=50\)): Observed empirical accuracy dominates with 83% weight, while shrinkage fades toward zero.

1. What It Measures

The student's historical success rate and practice volume exclusively on questions sharing the target question's specific skill tags.

2. Why It Matters

Single most predictive feature group in the entire pipeline (32.17% XGBoost gain). Skill mastery is highly localized.

3. Mathematical Formula

\[\tilde{A}_{\text{same}} = \frac{C_{\text{same}} + \alpha \cdot P_{\text{skill}}}{N_{\text{same}} + \alpha}, \quad L_{\text{same}} = \ln(1 + N_{\text{same}})\]

Where \(P_{\text{skill}}\) is the empirical base rate for that skill in the 80% training pool.

4. Concrete Numerical Example

Student with \(C_{\text{same}}=5\) out of \(N_{\text{same}}=8\) on Skill #27 (\(P=0.6289\)): \[\tilde{A}_{\text{same}} = \frac{5 + 10 \times 0.628947}{8 + 10} = \frac{11.2895}{18} = 0.6272, \quad L_{\text{same}} = \ln(9) = 2.1972\]

5. Dynamic Sensitivity ("What happens when...")

• First Encounter with Topic (\(N_{\text{same}}=0\)): Shrinks to skill-specific prior \(P_{\text{skill}}\), and \(L_{\text{same}} = 0.0\).
• Repeated Topic Errors: Feature drops rapidly below 0.50, strongly increasing predicted failure risk.
• Multi-Skill Target Items: Skill priors aggregate via fractional weighting (\(w = 1/|\mathcal{K}_q|\)).

1. What It Measures

The student's track record on all other skills excluding the current target skill(s), measuring transferable baseline competence.

2. Why It Matters

Disentangles general learning ability from topic-specific mastery. When a student enters a new topic ($N_{\text{same}}=0$), G3 signals whether they are fundamentally strong or struggling across the curriculum.

3. Mathematical Formula

\[\tilde{A}_{\text{other}} = \frac{C_{\text{other}} + \alpha \cdot P_{\text{other}}}{N_{\text{other}} + \alpha}\]

Where \(N_{\text{other}} = N_{\text{total}} - N_{\text{same}}\) and \(P_{\text{other}}\) is the training base rate of all non-target skills.

4. Concrete Numerical Example

Student with \(C_{\text{other}}=16\) out of \(N_{\text{other}}=22\) attempts (\(P_{\text{other}}=0.6586\)): \[\tilde{A}_{\text{other}} = \frac{16 + 10 \times 0.658558}{22 + 10} = \frac{22.5856}{32} = 0.7058 \quad (70.58\%)\]

5. Dynamic Sensitivity ("What happens when...")

• Only Target Skill Practiced (\(N_{\text{other}}=0\)): Shrinks gracefully to \(P_{\text{other}}\).
• Skill Transfer Scenario: High G3 indicates general capability even when G2 has zero exposure.
• Independence Note: \(P_{\text{other}} eq P_{\text{global}}\); overall accuracy and other-skill accuracy are never assumed to be identical.

1. What It Measures

The intrinsic difficulty of the upcoming problem (measured across all training-pool students), its exposure volume, and its presentation format.

2. Why It Matters

Accounts for question difficulty (17.54% XGBoost gain). A student is far more likely to fail an open-response question than an elementary multiple-choice item.

3. Mathematical Formula

\[\tilde{A}_q = \frac{C_q + \alpha \cdot P_{\text{global}}}{N_q + \alpha}, \quad L_q = \ln(1 + N_q)\]

Plus one-hot categorical encoding for answer_type.

4. Concrete Numerical Example

Problem #14052 with \(C_q=10\) correct out of \(N_q=14\) attempts: \[\tilde{A}_q = \frac{10 + 10 \times 0.658624}{14 + 10} = \frac{16.5862}{24} = 0.6911, \quad L_q = \ln(15) = 2.7081\]

5. Dynamic Sensitivity ("What happens when...")

• Unseen Problem (\(N_q=0\)): Shrinks to \(P_{\text{global}} = 0.658624\), preventing extreme difficulty assumptions.
• High-Volume Benchmark Questions: Empirically established difficulty dominates.
• Format Impact: open_response questions have lower baseline success rates than choose_1 multiple choice.

Multi-Skill Attribution Math & Empirical Shrinkage Derivation (Click to expand)

Multi-Skill Fractional Weighting: When a question addresses multiple skills \(\mathcal{K}_q = \{k_1, \dots, k_m\}\), credit is divided equally:

\[w_{s,q} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{K}_q|}, \quad C_k = \sum_{q \in \mathcal{H}} w_{s,q} \cdot y_{s,q}, \quad N_k = \sum_{q \in \mathcal{H}} w_{s,q}\]

Unseen Skill Fallback: If a test student encounters a skill with zero interactions in the 80% training pool, \(P_{\text{skill}}\) falls back to the uninformative prior \(0.50\).

Empirical Shrinkage Parameter: Smoothing strength \(\alpha = 10\) was tuned in cross-validation to prevent extreme probability distortions in the sparse early stages of student practice sequences.

5 Temporal Anti-Leakage Safeguards

Safeguard 1
Student Disjoint Split

\(\mathcal{S}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{S}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset\). No student's data is split across sets.

Safeguard 2
Chronological Lookback

Features computed strictly from \(\text{order\_id} < \text{order\_id}_{s,t}\). Zero future lookahead.

Safeguard 3
Training-Only Priors

\(P_{\text{G1}}=0.658624\) and skill priors calculated solely from the 80% training pool.

Safeguard 4
Training-Only Scaling

StandardScaler (\(\mu, \sigma\)) fit strictly on the training partition.

Safeguard 5
No Concurrent Features

Excluded 71,402 scaffolding sub-questions to eliminate concurrent leakage.

Cross-Validation Aggregation Math & Fold Dispersion Clarification (Click to expand)

Pooled OOF vs. Mean of Fold Recalls: The 75.00% figure represents the pooled out-of-fold recall calculated across all 219,983 concatenated validation predictions (\(56,322 / (56,322 + 18,775) = 0.74999 \approx 75.00\%\)). Across the five individual validation rounds, the fold-specific recalls are: Fold 1: 76.17%, Fold 2: 74.20%, Fold 3: 76.35%, Fold 4: 73.55%, Fold 5: 74.30%.

The unweighted mean of fold-specific recalls is 74.92% with a sample standard deviation of 1.26 percentage points across folds (\(\sigma = 0.0126\)).

Decision-Theoretic Threshold Selection: Operating threshold \(\tau = 0.2880\) prioritizes recall (75%) over precision (49.17%). In educational tutoring systems, the pedagogical cost of a false negative (a struggling student receives no support and suffers compounding frustration) far exceeds the cost of a false positive (a competent student is offered an optional hint).

Academic Scope & Context

Limitations, Team & Resources

Ethical considerations, pedagogical boundaries, course submission details, and reproducibility links.

Boundary 1

Observational Scope

Predictive associations identified in offline log data do not establish causal intervention mechanisms. Active tutoring interventions may alter student engagement and change the underlying data-generating distribution.

Boundary 2

Cold-Start Reliance

First-time students (\(N=0\)) rely heavily on population Bayesian priors (\(0.658624\)) until sufficient interaction evidence accumulates. Uncertainty is higher during early practice sequences.

Boundary 3

Domain Specificity

The pipeline is calibrated strictly on ASSISTments 2009–2010 middle-school mathematics. Transfer to other subject areas (e.g. language learning or programming) requires recalibration of empirical priors.
