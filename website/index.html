Theoretical Foundation

Problem Formulation

Formal mathematical definition of interaction-level outcome and failure risk prediction.

Academic Scope Notice: Next Move AI predicts the outcome of an individual question interaction on an online tutoring platform. It does not measure overall student intelligence, cumulative cognitive mastery, or grade-point average. Observational predictive relationships do not establish causal pedagogical mechanisms.

Sequence Formulation

Let \(s \in \mathcal{S}\) index a student and let \(t\) index their chronological interaction sequence, ordered globally by order_id.

  • Input Vector \(\mathbf{x}_{s,t}\): Constructed strictly from interactions prior to \(t\) (\(\text{order\_id} < \text{order\_id}_{s,t}\)).
  • Binary Target \(y_{s,t} \in \{0, 1\}\): Recorded outcome on the question (\(1 =\) correct, \(0 =\) non-correct).
  • Predicted Correct Probability:
    \[\hat{p}_{s,t} = P(y_{s,t} = 1 \mid \mathbf{x}_{s,t})\]
  • Complementary Failure Risk:
    \[r_{s,t} = 1 - \hat{p}_{s,t} = P(y_{s,t} = 0 \mid \mathbf{x}_{s,t})\]

Interaction-Level vs. Student-Level

Unlike static student proficiency ratings, interaction-level risk varies dynamically from question to question:

Low Predicted Risk (High Probability of Success)

Occurs when a student practices a familiar, mastered skill on an elementary or well-scaffolded problem.

High Predicted Risk (Potential Failure Trigger)

Occurs when even a high-performing student encounters a difficult, unfamiliar multi-skill problem (\(r_{s,t} \ge \tau\)).

Methodology & Leakage Prevention

Validation Strategy & Data Splitting

A three-stage, student-disjoint architecture guaranteeing zero identity leakage across cross-validation and held-out testing.

Evaluation Partitions:
Training Pool (80% / 4 Folds): Feature engineering, prior estimation, scaling & model training
Validation Folds (1 Fold Rotating): Out-of-fold scoring, model selection & threshold calibration
Held-Out Test Set (20% Isolated): Final unbiased evaluation; zero modeling exposure

Student-Level Train-Test Split

Initial 80:20 partition executed strictly at the student level using random_state=42. Partitions are completely student-disjoint to ensure genuine out-of-sample generalization to new learners.

× Flawed Protocol: Interaction-Level Random Split

Why Naive Row Splitting Causes Identity Leakage

In standard random splitting, individual rows are shuffled indiscriminately. This scatters past and future interactions from the same student across both training and test sets.

  • Student Identity Recognition: Because students possess stable underlying ability, the model memorizes idiosyncratic student tendencies rather than learning generalizable pedagogical dynamics.
  • Contaminated Histories: Features derived from a student's prior questions in the test fold leak information directly into their training rows.
  • Artificially Inflated Metrics: Validation performance looks deceptively high but collapses catastrophically when deployed to unseen learners.
✓ Rigorous Protocol: Student-Disjoint Split

Next Move AI: True Out-of-Sample Generalization

Next Move AI performs all data splits at the student level: \(\mathcal{S}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{S}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset\). An entire learner's trajectory is assigned to either training or testing.

  • Complete Learner Independence: All 844 test students are totally novel to the model; it has never seen a single prior question from any of them.
  • Inductive Generalization: The model must infer student competence on the fly using Bayesian priors and observable chronological momentum.
  • Honest Deployment Readiness: Evaluates exactly how an intelligent tutoring system will perform when an enrolled student logs in for the first time.

Stage 3: Final Model Training & Test Workflow

A strict boundary governs how cross-validation and test partitions were utilized:

  • Model Selection via CV: 5-fold cross-validation compared Logistic Regression, Random Forest, LightGBM, and XGBoost. XGBoost achieved top ranking discrimination (0.7477 ROC-AUC) and calibration (0.5475 Log Loss).
  • Threshold Calibration via CV: The operating decision threshold \(\tau = 0.2880\) was tuned strictly on 219,983 out-of-fold validation predictions to target 75% recall of non-correct responses.
  • Full Training-Pool Refitting: Once all modeling decisions were locked, the final XGBoost pipeline was refit on all 3,373 training-pool students (219,983 interactions).
  • Quarantined Test Evaluation: The held-out test set (844 students, 55,475 interactions) was evaluated exactly once. It was never used for feature selection, hyperparameter tuning, or threshold setting.

5 Temporal Anti-Leakage Safeguards

Within each partition, five strict algorithmic safeguards prevent lookahead bias and target self-contamination.

1. Chronological Sorting

All records sorted globally by user_id and ascending order_id to guarantee temporal validity.

2. Strict Past-Only Windows

Only interactions satisfying \(\text{order\_id} < \text{order\_id}_{s,t}\) are permitted to enter feature accumulators.

3. 1-Step Cumulative Lag

Correct counters implement cumsum().shift(1, fill_value=0) so the target interaction never enters its own history.

4. Attempt Count Exclusion

attempt_count reflects post-submission behavior and is strictly excluded to prevent outcome leakage.

5. Question Difficulty Isolation

The target student's own attempts on a problem are subtracted from question difficulty counters to eliminate self-leakage.

Information Architecture

Feature Engineering & Bayesian Smoothing

Four distinct information groups engineered with Bayesian m-estimate shrinkage.

Group 1

Overall Student History

Global track record across all previous eligible main questions regardless of subject. Selected: overall_smoothed_accuracy.

Group 2

Same-Skill History

Historical track record sharing target skill(s). Selected: weighted_same_skill_smoothed_accuracy and log experience.

Group 3

Other-Skill History

Performance on questions in non-overlapping skills, capturing cross-domain baseline ability. Selected: weighted_other_skill_smoothed_accuracy.

Group 4

Question Difficulty & Format

Empirical success rates across other students plus categorical question type. Selected: smoothed difficulty, log volume, and format.

Benchmark Comparison

Baseline Model (V0)

Evaluating performance without student-specific historical feature engineering.

Global Prior Benchmark Mechanism

In each cross-validation fold, the baseline computes the training-fold empirical correct response rate (\(\bar{p}_{\text{train}} \approx 0.6586\), where \(\pm 0.0031\) represents the standard deviation of baseline priors across the five training folds, and \(0.658624\) represents the full 80% training-pool prior) and predicts that constant probability for every single validation interaction.

Because predictions are identical constants across all items, discrimination metrics like ROC-AUC were not calculated in the baseline notebook. Evaluation focuses on proper scoring rules (Log Loss and Brier Score).
